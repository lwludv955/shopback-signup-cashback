## What ShopBack Actually Is

The way it funds the cashback is the part most people get wrong on first guess. ShopBack doesn't mark up your price at checkout. You pay the same price you'd pay arriving at the store directly. The cashback comes out of the affiliate commission the merchant pays to anyone who refers a paying customer — deal blogs, comparison sites, browser extensions, cashback apps. ShopBack registers as one of those referrers and passes most of that commission back to you as real cash.

Three things fall out of that model, and they're worth internalizing before you sign up:

1. **You pay the same price at checkout.** Cashback comes from the merchant's marketing budget, not from a hidden markup.
2. **The referral has to be tracked.** You have to click through from ShopBack so the merchant can attribute the sale. No click-through, no cashback.
3. **Cashback clears after the merchant validates the order.** It starts as "Pending" and becomes "Confirmed" once the store's return window closes, so a returned item doesn't reverse cashback you already withdrew.

## How Cashback Tracking Actually Works

This is the boring part that determines whether you actually get paid, so it's worth a few paragraphs.

When you click through from ShopBack to a merchant, a tracking cookie is set that lets the merchant attribute the sale to ShopBack. The merchant then pays ShopBack a commission, and ShopBack credits your share as cashback. Your order moves through three stages:

1. **Pending.** Usually within 48 hours of checkout, the order appears in your ShopBack activity as Pending. The tracking signal has been received.
2. **Confirmed.** Once the merchant validates the order — for retail, typically a few weeks; for travel, usually after the trip is completed — the cashback moves to Confirmed.
3. **Withdrawable.** Once your Confirmed Cashback balance hits $5, you can withdraw to PayPal or via ACH transfer. The daily withdrawal limit is $300, and withdrawals typically process within 10 calendar days (up to 30 in some cases).

To keep tracking reliable, a few habits help: disable ad-blockers and cashback-blockers on the merchant's checkout page, clear competing affiliate cookies before you start (or use a fresh browser session), and avoid coupon-site detours between clicking through from ShopBack and completing checkout. If a purchase doesn't track, you can file a missing-cashback claim in the app under **Account → Cashback → Missing Cashback** within the claim window listed on the merchant's page.

## How Cashback Is Calculated

Cashback is calculated on the rate displayed at each store, applied to the purchase subtotal. Most stores exclude shipping fees and taxes from the calculation. So if a store offers 5% cashback and your order subtotal is $100, you get $5 back in USD.

Rates vary by store, category, and campaign, and they change regularly. Boosted rates show up around major sale events — Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Prime Day — and ShopBack runs a recurring monthly travel campaign between the 18th and 23rd of each month, during which you can see up to 15% cashback on hotels via platforms like Trip.com and Expedia, and up to 13% on flights. Always verify the current rate on the specific store's ShopBack page before you buy.

**Rakuten** is the long-established US cashback service (formerly Ebates). If you value a long US track record and don't mind waiting for quarterly payouts, Rakuten is a solid pick.

**Honey** is primarily a coupon-code browser extension owned by PayPal. It tests known codes at checkout, which is a different job than cashback. Honey Gold is a limited points program on select merchants, not traditional cashback.

The honest answer is that these tools solve different problems and most savvy shoppers use a cashback service plus a coupon extension together. Start your session at ShopBack so the cashback tracking link is set, click through to the merchant, and let Honey or another coupon extension apply a working code at checkout. Both the code discount and the cashback land on the same order, in most cases.
